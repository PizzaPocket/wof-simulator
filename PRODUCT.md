# Wings of Fire game

This is a personal 3D Wings of Fire project in Godot, with world planning, an early playable dragon movement prototype, a world atlas and a detailed MudWing village. The current extension adds playable bare terrain across Pyrrhia, Pantala, the arc-four Forgotten Isles and NightWing Island. This record follows the existing [README](README.md) and the explicitly approved terrain scope; no commercial model or broader audience strategy is established here.

Opening the project with F5 starts the atlas. Players drag and zoom one world map, click a pin on any kingdom, island or shore (or pick it from a list), walk or fly, choose another starting point and return to the map. Pyrrhia retains its separate MudWing village marker, and a nearby entrance from its Mud Kingdom terrain start. Existing village characters and collection interactions remain part of that detailed destination.

The approved terrain foundation consists of basic colored ground, mountains, hills, coast and water with usable collision. It preserves source-map horizontal proportions. New vegetation, buildings, settlement scenery, NPCs and quests are outside this extension. The map and terrain lead the interface; existing native controls, marsh/gold/parchment colors and default Godot typography remain the visual authority. See [Overworld design](docs/overworld-design.md).

The four landmasses are separate playable scenes. World is an atlas overview using one metre scale and illustrative offsets, including NightWing Island northeast of the Sky Kingdom tip. It is not a seamless shared ocean. Pyrrhia now uses a 320 km width with actual disk streaming, a tenfold expansion of the original prototype; its roughly 2.5 km peaks retain their heights while mountain widths broaden fourfold. The streaming system was validated on Pyrrhia before the other maps were expanded. Pantala now uses a 320 km width; the Forgotten Isles and NightWing Island use 120 km and 100 km widths. These are tenfold expansions of the earlier gameplay sizes and remain provisional physical scales. Relative physical sizes, spacing, elevations and small terrain details are authored interpretations rather than canonical measurements. The Forgotten Isles uses the enlarged main island and nearby inset islands rather than inventing land for every distant map dot.

Pyrrhia streams nearby terrain from independently stored pages, keeps a small distant overview resident, and releases geometry behind the player. The terrain working budget is estimated separately from player assets and total engine/GPU memory. Pantala and the island maps now use the same bounded streaming approach after the Pyrrhia test passed. Geographic rules feed both atlas and terrain. Runtime assets are original geometry, heightfields and procedural biome colors; reference illustrations remain in ignored cache files. Deep-water recovery returns to the selected start; swimming is outside this pass. Implementation details, source references and verification limits are recorded in [Bare playable world terrain](docs/bare-terrain.md).

The project currently targets Godot 4.5+ with the native Mac renderer as the primary review target. Desktop and small-window atlas layouts are reviewed at 1280 × 800 and 800 × 500. Renderer captures and automated checks do not establish web-export performance or physical controller validation. Installation, model setup and controls remain in [the game guide](game/README.md).
